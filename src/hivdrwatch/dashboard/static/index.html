SAWA · open HIV-1 drug-resistance surveillance

Resistance calls on public HIV-1 pol sequences

SAWA is an open, reproducible pipeline that ingests public HIV-1 pol sequences, calls resistance with Stanford HIVdb Sierra, and publishes the denominator behind every figure. This page summarises one data vintage; it makes no live calls and is drawn from a committed JSON file.

The cohort is public HIV-1 pol sequences retrieved by an East-Africa-targeted LANL/GenBank query. That names the query, not the sequences. of ingested accessions returned a Sierra result ( did not), giving resistance calls across drugs in classes.

Resistance prevalence by drug class

A sequence counts as resistant to a class if any drug in it scores intermediate or high-level resistance in HIVdb. Classes are in manuscript order.

Treatment history is not recorded for these sequences. These are not pretreatment-resistance estimates: the corpus mixes surveillance, treatment-failure and trial sequences in unknown proportion, so the figures below should not be read against pretreatment drug-resistance surveys.

The denominator is “Sierra returned a score for this gene”. That is not the same as “the surveillance codons were sequenced”: the data carry no per-gene coordinates, so true codon coverage cannot be computed without re-calling Sierra, and a sequence that stops short of a codon counts as unmutated there. Only of sequences have integrase coverage, because most deposited pol sequences cover PR and RT only.

Every prevalence, level and coverage figure on this page counts accessions, and “sequences” means accessions. The scored accessions hold distinct sequences, so repeat a sequence already present under another accession. Patient identity is not recorded, so these are not independent patients.

Resistance by country and year

Each cell is one country in one collection year. Its colour is the share of the sequences in it that score intermediate or high-level resistance to any drug in the class, by the same rule as above, and the number of sequences it rests on is printed in it where there is room and given in its tooltip, or when you tap it. Rows are countries, columns are years, and the colour runs from pale at 0% to dark at 100% on one scale shared by all four classes. This view sums across subtypes: Paper 1’s Figure 2 stratifies by subtype and this page does not, so there is no subtype breakdown here even though the subtype field is now correct.

Cell-to-cell differences may reflect cohort composition rather than epidemiology. The corpus records no treatment history, so a pretreatment survey and a cohort of people already on treatment cannot be told apart, and they produce very different shares. Read each cell as a description of the sequences in it, not as a trend over time or a comparison between countries.

Sampling effort is not uniform. Cells at or above the floor hold between and sequences, and half of them hold or fewer, which is why each cell shows its own n. A share over a handful of sequences moves a long way with one more resistant sample, and that is the reason cells under the floor are left uncoloured.

    Surveillance resistance mutations

    Sierra reports every variant relative to consensus, and the most frequent are natural polymorphisms, not resistance. Only positions and states on the surveillance list (Bennett et al. 2009, updated 2019) are counted, for protease (PR) and reverse transcriptase (RT).

    • PR
    • RT, NRTI list

    Integrase mutations are not shown. The vendored surveillance list predates the dolutegravir era: Bennett et al. 2009 has no integrase component, and the integrase block in the reference file is the raltegravir/elvitegravir-era list, missing R263K, G118R and E138K. Showing it would attribute to Bennett a list it does not contain.

    Counting convention: a mixture counts as carrying a surveillance mutation when any of its states is one. Where a mixture matches more than one state, it is attributed to the alphabetically first matching state. That affects of observations ( of ). The effect falls on one side only: the earlier state’s count is correct, because the mixture does carry it, while the later state’s count is understated by the mixtures that also matched an earlier one.
